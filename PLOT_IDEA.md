# BARBARA: The Starshiners Record (Plot Idea)

*An analog-horror adaptation of* BARBARA *by Thananarkin Ekabut, in the style of* Noroi: The Curse *and* The Mandela Catalogue.

## Premise

A found-footage documentary assembled by an editor from recovered tapes. Documentary filmmaker **Ruth Calloway** set out to investigate a missing girl. She came back "in good health" with no memory of the project. Her camera operator, **Teo**, was never found. The viewer pieces together the clues and realizes that the **Starshiners Society** is quietly bringing about the end of the world.

## The Cult

- **Henotes** isn't a real god's name. It's the Greek word for *oneness*. It's a being made of the names people give it.
- The rite: **give your name, drink from the Krater, become "perfect."** Perfect really means *the same as everyone else*. Your self is absorbed into Henotes.
- It only targets **the different**: people with an unusual voice or face, outsiders, the lonely. *Barbaros* is the Greek root of "barbarian," meaning someone whose voice sounds wrong.
- **The apocalypse:** once the last outsider is absorbed, nobody is different anymore. Henotes is complete. Nobody dies and nobody notices. Everyone just agrees, forever. The world sees it as good news: loneliness and bullying fall to zero.

## Plot

1. **The farm, 1986.** A vet's tape: a black sheep bursts open and gives birth to a perfect white lamb that isn't hers. This is the first assimilation. The farm later becomes the Starshiners "clinic."
2. **The email.** Barbara's friend **Katie**, who has the same deep voice, writes to Ruth: "My best friend is gone, but she's still here." Barbara never went missing on paper. A beautiful new girl, **Zora**, sits in her seat. Nobody else remembers the old Barbara, and even the yearbook photo has changed.
3. **The evidence.**
   - A box of Barbara's things holds the *Barbaros and the Star* cartoon VHS. Copies only ever turn up in waiting rooms at speech-therapy and dermatology clinics.
   - Her phone holds a recording of her call to **Emma**. After she hangs up, the recording keeps going, and a crowd chants her name.
   - The phone number can't exist, but it always connects.
   - Starshiners ads span 30 years with the same voice and a face that matches pixel for pixel. That face also matches Zora, Bella and Emma.
   - The same essay praising conformity has won school prizes in 1995, 2009 and 2017.
4. **The scholar.** Adam's 1998 lecture explains the rite. When Ruth finds him in a care home, he warns her: "They can only take what is *given*. Don't say your name into that camera." Weeks later he makes a "miraculous recovery."
5. **The ex-member.** "Lena" only drank twice and escaped. She writes her real name on her hand every morning. She describes **the Constellation**: at night, the assimilated stand in rows in the barn like sheep.
6. **The call.** Ruth calls the number. Emma already knows her name, because Ruth has said it on every interview slate.
7. **The clinic.** The cult's ledger holds Ruth's name 61 times, in her own handwriting, and Katie's name is next. Hundreds of the assimilated stand in the barn, including Barbara and Adam. They turn and welcome Ruth home. Ruth walks to the Krater willingly. Teo escapes, but he forgets his own name while driving and walks away.
8. **After.** Ruth appears on local TV, radiant, promoting "Become One." Teo leaves a voicemail warning: "It only needs the different ones." Mid-sentence his voice turns perfect and he asks for the listener's name.
9. **The last tape.** Katie is now the only deep voice left at school. She watches the hidden Part II of the cartoon, which reveals that **Hermes was Henotes all along**. She calls the number: "My name is Katelyn—"
10. **Ending.** A final ad shows a white flock turning toward one black sheep: "Still the only one? You don't have to be." Every name in the credits dissolves into one name, **HENOTES**, and then one word, **ἕν** ("one").

## Theme

The original script's *conformity vs. individualism*, taken literally. The monster's promise is that **no one will ever be lonely again**.
