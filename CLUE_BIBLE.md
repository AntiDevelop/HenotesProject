# CLUE BIBLE: *BARBARA: The Starshiners Record*

A behind-the-scenes reference for the writer and director. It lists the cult's rules, the timeline, and every clue planted in the film, so the mystery stays consistent when it's revised or shot.

---

## 1. What is actually happening

**Henotes** (Greek ἑνότης, "oneness") is not really a god and has no name of its own. It is a **singularity made of offered names**. Each person who gives their name to the **Krater** and drinks becomes part of it. Their body is made "perfect," meaning *the same as everyone else's*, and their self is absorbed.

It only targets **the different**: people with unusual voices, faces, or bodies; outsiders; the lonely. The beautiful and the normal are already "the same," so it doesn't need to touch them.

**The apocalypse is the logical end of this:** once the last outsider is absorbed, there is no difference left anywhere. Henotes becomes whole and "says the only name." Nobody dies. Nobody notices. The world just stops having strangers. It ends with everyone agreeing forever. It's the original script's theme, *conformity vs. individualism*, taken literally.

The horror of it is that **everyone experiences it as good news**: loneliness drops, bullying drops, self-esteem rises.

## 2. The rules of the cult (keep these consistent)

| Rule | Where it's shown |
|---|---|
| A name has to be **given**: spoken, written, or signed. It can't be taken. | Adam: "They can only take what is given." |
| Recording your name counts as giving it. | Emma's call to Ruth; the slates; the ledger with 61 "Ruth Calloway" lines |
| 1st drink = face. 2nd = voice. 3rd = name. | Lena's interview |
| After assimilation, **reality edits itself**: memories, photos and records shift to match. | Yearbook photo printed *before* Barbara called; Amanda doesn't recognize the old Barbara |
| The assimilated share **one face** (Emma = Sophia = Bella = Zora = the ad girl = Henotes's proportions). | The overlay scenes |
| Tells: **perfectly straight posture**, a pause like buffering before answering, moving in sync, unison laughter. | Emma, the nurse, Amanda, Ruth on TV, the dismissal footage |
| At night the finished "stars" come back to the barn and stand in rows, **facing one way like sheep**. This is "the Constellation." | Lena; the clinic climax |
| The Society always **discloses the truth** in its own media (the cartoon, the essay, the ads). Adam: *"Deceit with full disclosure is the devil's courtesy."* | The Part II tape tells Katie everything, and she calls anyway |
| The phone number **cannot exist** (a "111" area code isn't possible, and it has only 9 digits) but always connects. | Garrity interview |

**The one weakness:** keep your name to yourself. Lena survives, only partly, by writing her real name on her hand every morning. Joan survives (for now) by **not telling her own sister her name**. Notice that Joan's full first name never appears anywhere in the film. Keep it that way.

## 5. Recurring motifs

- **Sheep / the flock.** The black sheep is the outsider. White sheep in rows are the assimilated. "The flock does not know it is a flock."
- **Light vs. shadow.** Barbaros hides in shadow, and "light drowns out all the darkness" in the ad. The "Unity" comes when there is *no shadow left to hide in*. Light is the threat in this story, not the safe place.
- **Names.** Track every time someone says or writes a full name. Joan's is the only one never given.
- **Mirrors.** The ad girl, Barbara's bathroom, Katie's bathroom (framed exactly the same on purpose), the krater's reflecting surface.
- **"Greetings, friends. My name is ___."** Barbara's presentation card, Zora's livestream, and Barbaros at the krater all stall at the same point: the name.
- **The deep voice.** *Barbaros* is the Greek root of "barbarian": someone whose speech sounds wrong. Barbara and Katie are literal barbaroi.
- **The circle with a dot.** The symbol on the medical form. It's an ancient sign for unity: one point, everything around it.
- **Inscription:** *Ὄνομα δός. Πίε. Ἓν γενοῦ.* "Give your name. Drink. Become one."

## 6. Why it feels real (production notes)

- Base everything in **paperwork**: invoices, deeds, school newspapers, telecom tickets, yearbooks. Analog horror works because the documents look boring.
- Use **real-sounding but fictional** names for institutions (Northfield Regional High, Lyceum Kids Video, St. Agnes Care Home, "NH Tonight – Channel 9"). Real towns (Concord, Wentworth, Plymouth, Bow, Lowell) are fine as settings.
- **Never show Henotes in live action.** Only the cartoon, the mural, and the face overlay. What the audience pieces together is scarier than anything you could show.
- Keep the assimilated **kind**. They never threaten anyone. They welcome you. That's what makes them frightening.
- Let the **editor's title cards** carry the dread. They should sound clinical, sad, and short.
- Sound design: when the stars speak in unison, record one actor and layer them to 100–400 voices, perfectly in sync. Emma's voice should be one take of one actress, reused **exactly** across 1994, 2003, 2016 and 2024. The audience will hear that it's identical even if they can't say why.

## 7. Changes from the original *BARBARA* script

| Original | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| A linear story about Barbara | Barbara's story is **reconstructed** from evidence by an outside investigator |
| The cartoon cuts in as narrative interludes | The cartoon is a **physical VHS artifact** with a provenance, and its hidden "Part II" is the final reveal |
| The Starshiners ad appears once | The ad repeats across **30 years** with an identical face and voice |
| Adam's lecture | Kept word for word as a 1998 tape, plus a present-day follow-up interview. He is later assimilated too |
| Bella, Amanda, Emma, Sophia, the Surgeon | Kept, and recast as **clues** (identical faces, posture, the essay) |
| The bathroom soliloquy | Kept word for word as Barbara's last recorded video, then **mirrored** by Katie at the end |
| Hermes asks "Do you know what that Krater contains?" (cliffhanger) | Answered in Part II. **Hermes was Henotes all along.** |
| Ends with Barbara assimilated | Ends with the **apocalypse spreading**: Ruth, Teo, Adam and Katie are absorbed, and the viewer is addressed directly |

All original dialogue from Thananarkin Ekabut's script that could be kept has been kept verbatim, so it still reads as an adaptation of their work.
